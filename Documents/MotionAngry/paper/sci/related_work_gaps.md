# Gaps this paper fills (tied to the cited works)

> 실험 뒤 상태(2026-10-30 22:30, docs/SELF_REVIEW.md 반영): 항목 2의 '이기는 영역'은 비어 있고(p*는 없음), 항목 3의 '사용자 안 판별 불변'은 실제로는 '저하'로 읽어야 하며, 항목 6의 '대체 응답 수'는 산출하지 않았고(백로그), 항목 7의 가중치 분석은 멤버십 추론이 아니라 최근접 재식별(82%)로 수행했다. 본문(paper/sci/main.md)이 기준이다.

Each bullet names what prior work did, what it did not do, and what our paper contributes. Keys match `references.bib`.

1. **Cross-modal face-to-movement self-training, running on the phone.** Albanie et al. distilled a facial-expression teacher into a speech student offline on a large video corpus [Albanie2018]; Ni et al. distilled video into accelerometer for action recognition [Ni2022]; Onim et al. used facial-expression software as labels for a wearable physiological model trained offline in a lab [Onim2025]. None of these (a) uses smartphone movement features as the student, (b) performs the update on the device, hourly, from the user's own data, or (c) evaluates against a ground truth that is independent of the teacher. Our system does all three, with a 33-parameter LiteRT student and a random-time Affect Grid arm as the only ground truth.

2. **Teacher accuracy and label density as controlled variables, not a single operating point.** Every cross-modal and self-training result above reports one teacher at one density. Noisy-label theory predicts a trade-off (information per label scales with 2p-1, learnability requires p > 0.5, sample complexity grows as 1/(1-2eta)^2) [Angluin1988; Frenay2014], but it has not been measured for affect pseudo-labels. Our loop simulator replays K-EmoPhone [Kang2023] with teacher accuracy p and density d swept jointly, and reports the (p, d) region in which a self-trained student matches or exceeds the global model. This turns the honest negative result (no within-user gain with about one week of data, even with oracle labels) into a stated condition: the gain that Taylor et al. and Meegahapola et al. obtained with weeks to months of labels [Taylor2020; Meegahapola2022] does not appear at K-EmoPhone's data volume, and the simulator shows how much density would be required.

3. **Within-user versus pooled gains reported separately.** Fisher et al. argue that group-level and individual-level effects are different quantities [Fisher2018], and EmoPhone shows the best method changes between within-user and cross-user settings [Zhang2026], yet personalization papers typically report one aggregate metric. We report within-user AUROC and pooled AUROC side by side and show that on-device fine-tuning improves pooled AUROC (per-user level calibration) while leaving within-user discrimination unchanged. That distinction is absent from [Wampfler2022; Woll2026; Taylor2020] and is the reason our headline result reads as calibration, not discrimination.

4. **A performance guard evaluated as a statistical test, including its false-revert rate.** On-device and continual learning systems [Kwon2023; Cai2020] and personalized federated learning [Tan2023] assume the update is beneficial or validate it on held-out labelled data, which an unsupervised on-device learner does not have. We model the guard as a Bernoulli comparison against the global model on random probes and show that a fixed-threshold guard reverts a student that is exactly as good as the baseline 48% of the time at delta = 0. The same simulation gives the probe counts and margins at which false-revert and false-keep rates fall to usable levels. No prior self-training or on-device learning paper we found quantifies its guard this way.

5. **Measuring, rather than acknowledging, selection bias from model-triggered prompts.** Lathia et al. showed that sensor-triggered ESM distorts the sampled behaviour distribution [Lathia2013], Reiter and Schoedel showed non-response is itself non-random [Reiter2024], and inverse-probability weighting [Horvitz1952] and randomized timing [Klasnja2015] are the standard fixes. Affect-sensing papers that trigger prompts from a model still evaluate on those prompts. We keep a random-time arm as the only evaluation ground truth and use the triggered arm only to measure how far model-selected moments depart from the random-time distribution in arousal, valence and movement level (with IPW-style reweighting where the trigger probability is known).

6. **Density-versus-burden placed on the same axis as density-versus-noise.** ESM research quantifies burden [Eisele2022; vanBerkel2017] and semi-supervised affect work reduces label demand [Yu2023; Tang2021], but the two are not connected. Our (p, d) map lets a designer read the number of self-reports per day that the face teacher substitutes for at a given teacher accuracy, which is the quantity needed to decide whether a camera-based teacher is worth its cost.

7. **Privacy of both the summary features and the updated weights, for the same system.** Prior work shows aggregated accelerometry re-identifies people [Na2018; Neverova2016; Kroger2019], anonymizes features on-device [Malekzadeh2019], or analyses leakage from model parameters and updates [Shokri2017; Melis2019; Abadi2016; Benouis2024]. No study we found measures both for a deployed on-device affect model. We report closed-set re-identification from our 32 summary features (7% on 77 users) and the attribute and membership information recoverable from the 33 hourly-updated logistic weights (the weight-leakage analysis is planned; numbers must be filled in from the privacy experiment), and state the conditions (closed set, seven days, no demographics) under which the low numbers hold.

8. **A negative result stated as a precondition.** Taken together, items 2 to 4 reframe the finding that one week of data does not yield within-user gains. It is consistent with [Zhang2026]'s conclusion that participant variability, not only label scarcity, limits mobile affect sensing, and it specifies for the first time the teacher accuracy, label density and guard sample size that an on-device self-training loop needs before it can be expected to help an individual user.
